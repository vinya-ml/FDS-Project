Abstract— Agri metaverse Intelligence is an advanced online 
predictive model that is grounded on machine learning 
algorithms and advanced statistical models to predict the prices 
of 22 agricultural commodities. The system produces the correct 
five-year forecasts (2025-2029) based on past price data (2014- 
2024) and external economic factors and environmental factors. 
It is a decision support application using the Streamlit 
framework, a variety of time series models, including ensemble 
models, ARIMA, SARIMAX, and Prophet, to improve the 
quality and strength of forecasts and minimize financial risks to 
strategic agricultural economic planning through quantitative 
and evidence-based forecasting. It is a user-friendly, beginner 
easily to understand and dashboard allowing the stakeholders, 
which are agricultural traders, government policymakers, 
farmer cooperatives, and financial institutions, to access 
accurate and data-driven forecasts to predict commodity prices 
and evaluate financial risks. 
Keywords— Agricultural Forecasting, Commodity Prices, 
ARIMA, SARIMAX, Prophet, Time Series Analysis, Seasonal 
Decomposition, Price Prediction, Machine Learning. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
The volatility of the price of agricultural commodities is a 
danger to food security, the well-being of the farmers and 
economic stability in the modern world. Price uncertainty is 
characterized by changes in prices, turbulence in the market 
and deteriorating financial losses as time goes by eventually 
leading to income instability and sub-optimal decisions by the 
participants in the agricultural sector [1]. Therefore, it is 
important to make timely and correct price forecasts to make 
trading decisions and take measures to optimize the allocation 
of resources and the quality of life, both to farmers and 
agribusiness organizations. 
Deep learning and time series techniques incorporating 
transfer learning and the inclusion of variables have 
demonstrated significant potential for accurately forecasting 
agricultural commodity prices based on past price data. 
Numerous multi-algorithm strategies offer possibilities to 
reuse and apply pre-existing statistical and machine learning 
models thereby minimizing model training duration and while 
enhancing model precision [2] [3]. Ensemble forecasting has 
recently demonstrated its effectiveness in forecasting prices 
by utilizing multiple pretrained and fine-tuned models, like 
ARIMA, SARIMAX and Prophet [4]. 
The ongoing study aims at offering an understanding on the 
application of different time series methods in predicting the 
prices of agricultural commodities. In this work some 
forecasting models were. Their comparisons in terms of 
ARIMA, SARIMAX and Prophet. Their performance was 
evaluated based on the metrics, including R2 score, RMSE, 
MAPE and directional accuracy among others. Consequently, 
SARIMAX became the performing model, to predict 
agricultural prices in terms of the evaluation results. 
We have improved the forecasting structure by adding 
hyperparameter 
optimization 
and 
data 
preprocessing 
algorithms to obtain much better accuracy in predicting the 
price of agricultural commodities. The resulting models were 
applied to the SIH dataset of 22 commodities over 11 years 
with an R2 of 0.756 of SARIMAX and all three models with 
statistical significance of p=0.012. The results of this study 
demonstrate how the principles of transfer learning can be 
used and factors included to enhance the accuracy of time 
series 
models, 
in 
predicting 
prices 
of 
agricultural 
commodities. 
The implications of this study are as follows since the 
prediction of agricultural prices is essential to successful 
trading, crop management and policymaking. By using these 
techniques, it is easy to develop models that enable more 
precise price predictions which can increase the revenues of 
farmers and make markets more efficient [6]. Moreover, the 
inclusion of economic variables and seasonal decomposition 
can reduce the uncertainty in price forecasts and hence make 
these models more useful to scholars and industry 
practitioners [7]. Overall, this study can be added to the 
existing body of knowledge on time series modelling to 
predict prices and provide useful information to improve the 
performance of agricultural markets and, in turn, benefit the 
stakeholders.

III. METHODOLOGY 
A. Dataset and its Characteristics 
The agricultural commodity price data is historical data 
collected by the SIH (Series, Index, Historical) database 
covering the period between 2014 and 2024 with a total of 11 
years of data. Overall, it includes prices for 22 farm products 
categorized into grains (rice, wheat, atta) pulses (gram dal, tur 
dal, moong dal, urad dal, masoor dal) oils (groundnut oil, 
mustard oil, soya oil, sunflower oil, vanaspati, palm oil) 
vegetables (potato, onion, tomato) and additional staples such, 
as sugar, milk, tea, gur and salt. Prices are annual average 
prices in Indian Rupees (₹) per unit, usually per kilogram or 
per Liter. 
Additionally, the dataset includes 13 constructed external 
feature variables depicting weather patterns economic 
indicators and policy elements. These comprise: Rainfall (mm) 
Average Temperature (, °C) Drought Index, GDP Growth Rate 
(%) Inflation Rate (%) USD/INR Exchange Rate, Crude Oil 
Price ($/barrel) Fertilizer Price Index, Fuel Price (₹/Liter) 
Minimum Support Price Index, Export Volume Index, Storage 
Capacity Index and Transportation Cost Index. 
 
The dataset features can be outlined as follows: 
Commodity count: 22 durations: 11 years total entries:242(22 
commodities × 11 years) training span: 8 years (20142021) 
validation span: 2 years (2022-2023) testing duration: 1 year. 
Data was used to verify labels, by Indian government 
agricultural statistics and the data were compiled using multiple 
valid sources. This data is supposed to establish an accurate 
model to predict the price of agricultural commodities which 
would have a practical value in improving the accuracy of price 
projections and intended to aid in making informed decisions, in 
agricultural trade and policy formulation.

C. Preprocess Data for Analysis 
1. Normalization: 
During data preparation prices and external factors were 
standardized to a scale to maintain consistency and facilitate 
analysis. Variations in ranges and scales present in the data 
could affect the performance of the time series models. To 
convert all variables into the input formats for the models 
applied in this research every price and feature was adjusted 
to fall, between 0 and 1 using min-max normalization. 
2. Handling Missing Values: 
Due to the absence of historical price data, a forward- 
filling approach was applied; the prior year’s value was taken 
as a substitute for the absent years. This technique preserves 
the temporal continuity, in time series while minimizing 
distortion to maintain data accuracy.

VI. 
CONCLUSION 
This paper introduces Agri metaverse Intelligence- a price 
prediction system of agricultural commodities that uses three 
synergistic time series techniques combined with systematic 
comparative studies across a wide spectrum of commodities. The 
framework created provides predictive accuracy for 22 
accuracies of 22 agricultural commodities based on 11 years of 
past data and with evidence-based model selection and strong 
statistical validation. 
Its key contributions are: (i) creation and optimization of 
three complementary time series forecasting models - ARIMA, 
SARIMAX and Prophet - and an empirical evaluation of the 
models on a range of commodities; (ii) rigorous statistical testing 
that establishes the ranking of performance and confirms the 
significance of methodological differences at p = 0.012; (iii) the 
inclusion of 13 external factors, including economic, weather, 
and policy data, which have delivered significant improvements 
in accuracy over single variable models; (iv) launch of an 
intuitive web-based platform that makes advanced forecasting 
tools accessible, to farmers, traders and policymakers; 
commodity-specific modelling guidance derived from empirical 
performance assessments; and a statistical validation framework 
that supports reliable comparison and selection of models. 
The future will be characterized by real-time data 
assimilation regular forecasts that allow finer market responses, 
integration of alternative data, including satellite
